Recognising airliners from the ground

Seen from below, many aircraft look alike. Yet a few details are often enough to recognise them, even from far away. Here are the clues spotters use, from the easiest to the subtlest.

1. Count the engines and look at where they are

  • Two engines under the wings: the vast majority of today's airliners (Airbus A320 family, Boeing 737, A350, 787, 777…).
  • Four engines: increasingly rare, mostly widebodies (Airbus A380, Boeing 747) or military transports (A400M, C-130).
  • Engines at the rear of the fuselage: business jets and older regional jets (Embraer ERJ, Bombardier CRJ).
  • Propellers: turboprop regional aircraft such as the ATR 72 (wings on top of the fuselage) or the Dash 8.

2. Size and number of aisles

A single-aisle aircraft (A320, 737) is about 35 to 45 m long; a widebody (A350, 777, 787) is 57 to 75 m, with very large engines. From afar, a widebody looks slow and massive, and its engines seem almost as large as the fuselage is wide.

3. Airbus A320 or Boeing 737?

This is the most common question, as these two families make up a large share of flights in Europe.

  • The nose: round and even on the A320; more pointed, with a “chin”, on the 737.
  • The engines: on classic 737s, the engine intake is flattened at the bottom because the engine sits very close to the ground. On the 737 MAX, the engine exhausts have saw-tooth edges (chevrons).
  • The wingtips: recent A320s (A320neo, “A20N” in Plane GO) have large upturned winglets, the “sharklets”; recent 737s have split or steeply raked winglets.
  • The tail cone: the A320 ends in a well-centred point, the 737 in a flatter cone.

4. Widebodies

  • Boeing 787: very flexible wings that bend upwards in flight, chevron engines, a rounded nose with no “corner” at the cockpit.
  • Airbus A350: blade-shaped upturned wingtips, black outline around the cockpit windows (the “mask”).
  • Boeing 777: six wheels on each main landing gear leg, knife-edge tail cone, huge engines.
  • Airbus A380: two full-length decks and four engines: impossible to mistake.

5. Details that give away a rare aircraft

An unusual livery, a military shape (grey camouflage, refuelling probe), a very old aircraft with thin, noisy engines… In Plane GO these aircraft earn more XP: they are ranked from “common” to “legendary” by rarity.
