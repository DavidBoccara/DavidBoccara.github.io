Photographing aircraft: settings and good practice

An aircraft moves fast and is often far away: it is a demanding subject. With a few simple settings, you can still get sharp photos, even with a phone. Above all, photograph within the rules.

First, the rules

Aircraft photography is a hobby enjoyed by thousands of people, but it has clear limits:

  • From a public place: street, park, viewpoint or terrace open to the public. Do not enter private property without permission, nor airport grounds, behind a fence or into a restricted area.
  • Where photography is allowed: being allowed somewhere does not mean being allowed to take photos there. Some military or sensitive sites, and some airport areas, forbid it: follow the signs and the staff's instructions.
  • People: avoid photographing recognisable people without their consent.
  • Safety: never play while driving, and never stand on a road or near a runway.

In Plane GO, every player agrees to these rules before adding their first photo.

The settings that matter

  • Fast shutter speed: 1/1000 s or faster for a jet, to freeze the motion. For a propeller aircraft you can go down to about 1/250 s to keep the propellers blurred, which looks nicer, but you must then pan very smoothly.
  • Continuous autofocus (AF-C or AI Servo): the camera keeps adjusting focus while you follow the aircraft.
  • Burst mode: take several shots in a row and keep the best one.
  • Exposure compensation: against a bright sky the aircraft often looks too dark. Add +0.7 to +1.3 to brighten it.
  • Focal length: a 200 to 400 mm telephoto lens is ideal for aircraft in flight. On a phone, prefer the optical zoom (×3, ×5) to digital zoom.

Light and location

With the sun behind you, colours are vivid and details sharp. Mornings and late afternoons give softer light. Under an overcast sky the light is flat but even: photos lack contrast but remain usable.
